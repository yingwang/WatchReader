Start here

This short guide comes with WatchReader on your phone and on your watch. It shows you how to use the app and gives you a few pages to try the controls on.

Adding books

Add books on your phone. Tap the plus button in the library to open Add book, then choose from a file, a link, or Free books. Common formats are txt, epub and fb2. You can also share a book to WatchReader from another app.

Tap Free books to browse classics from Project Gutenberg, searchable by title, author, and language; they are public domain in the US. If you have no books of your own yet, tap any of the three free classics offered in your library to add it and send it to your watch.

Sending a book to the watch

Open the book on your phone and tap the send button at the top. You can also tap the three dots on the cover in your library and choose Send to watch.

Once sent, the watch has its own copy and reads it without the phone nearby.

Reading on the watch

Tap the book in the watch library to open it. Next time, opening the app on the watch goes straight back into the book you were reading.

Tap the right half of the page to go forward, or the left half to go back. Turn the crown to move one page per notch: turn it away from you to go forward, towards you to go back.

Press and hold the page to open the reading controls. Switch on Auto page turn to let pages turn by themselves at a pace set in Settings. Scroll down and tap "Back to book" to close the controls.

Listening

Press and hold the page and tap the triangle to start reading aloud from the page you are on. The watch reads the book to you, each sentence in the language it is written in, highlighting the sentence and turning the pages as it goes. Reading keeps going with the screen off or after you leave the app.

Double-tap the page while reading to pause; to carry on, press and hold the page and tap the triangle again. Open the reading controls and tap the square to stop, or use pause and stop in the notification.

Jumping to a chapter

Press and hold the page to open the reading controls, then tap Contents to list the chapters. Tap any chapter to jump straight there.

Pick a percentage with plus and minus, then tap "Go to this position" to jump there. Tap "Return to before jump" to go back.

Reading on the phone

Tap a book in the phone library to read it there. Tap the right side of the page to go forward, the left side to go back, and the middle to show or hide the bar at the top.

Tap the list icon at the top to open the contents and jump to a chapter or slider position. Your reading place is kept in step with the watch.

Below is a passage in the public domain, so you have real typesetting to look at and a few paragraphs to hear read aloud.

Pride and Prejudice, the first chapter

It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife.

However little known the feelings or views of such a man may be on his first entering a neighbourhood, this truth is so well fixed in the minds of the surrounding families, that he is considered as the rightful property of some one or other of their daughters.

"My dear Mr. Bennet," said his lady to him one day, "have you heard that Netherfield Park is let at last?"

Mr. Bennet replied that he had not.

"But it is," returned she; "for Mrs. Long has just been here, and she told me all about it."

Mr. Bennet made no answer.

"Do you not want to know who has taken it?" cried his wife impatiently.

"You want to tell me, and I have no objection to hearing it."

This was invitation enough.

"Why, my dear, you must know, Mrs. Long says that Netherfield is taken by a young man of large fortune from the north of England; that he came down on Monday in a chaise and four to see the place, and was so much delighted with it that he agreed with Mr. Morris immediately; that he is to take possession before Michaelmas, and some of his servants are to be in the house by the end of next week."

"What is his name?"

"Bingley."

"Is he married or single?"

"Oh! single, my dear, to be sure! A single man of large fortune; four or five thousand a year. What a fine thing for our girls!"

That is the end of the sample. To delete it on the watch, press and hold the book in the library and choose Delete, which removes only the watch's copy. On the phone, press and hold the book's cover, or tap the three dots on it, and choose Delete, which removes it from the phone and the watch. Now add a book you want to read.
