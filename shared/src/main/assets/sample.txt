Start here

This short guide comes with the app, on the phone and on the watch. It explains both, and it doubles as something to try every control on. When you are done with it, you can delete it at any time.

Reading on the phone

Tap a book in the library and it opens here, laid out a page at a time. Tap the right third of the page to go forward, the left third to go back, and the middle third to show or hide the title bar. A long press on the page also brings the title bar back. Your place is kept, so the book opens where you left it.

Three controls sit in the top right of the title bar. Contents opens a sheet with a percentage slider, and the chapter list appears when the book has chapters; this guide's own sections are listed there. Send to watch copies the book to your watch; once sent, a tick appears, and the watch reads its own copy without needing the phone nearby. The three dots menu lets you open Appearance to choose a dark, light or sepia page colour and adjust text size, or hide the title bar.

Reading on the watch

Opening WatchReader on the watch takes you straight back into the book you are reading. Tap the right half of the screen to go forward and the left half to go back. The crown does the same thing: turn it away from you to go forward, towards you to go back, one page for each notch.

Press and hold anywhere on the page to open the reading controls. They stay open while you use them. Here you can switch on auto page turn, which turns pages by itself at a pace chosen in Settings. Scroll down and tap Back to book when you are ready to carry on.

Your place travels with you. Read a few pages on the watch and the phone opens at the same spot, and the other way round, so you can put the phone down and carry on from your wrist.

Reading aloud

Press and hold the page to open the reading controls, then tap the triangle to have the watch read to you with the system speech engine. The sentence being spoken is highlighted and pages turn automatically to follow the voice. Reading carries on when the screen turns off and when you leave the app. Double-tap the page to pause, or use the pause button in the reading controls or notification. To resume, tap the triangle in the controls or Resume in the notification. To stop, tap the square in the controls or Stop in the notification.

You never have to choose a voice. Each sentence is spoken in the language it is written in, across 26 languages, so a book in English and a book in Chinese both read correctly, and a book that mixes the two switches as it goes. If the watch is missing a voice for a language, the speech engine downloads it automatically over Wi-Fi the first time a book needs it, wherever the speech engine offers one. You can check voice status and adjust reading speed in Settings under Read aloud.

Contents and jumps

Open Contents in the reading controls to jump straight to a chapter. Chapters are taken from an EPUB table of contents, FB2 sections, or headings detected in plain text. Below Contents, you can jump by percentage: each press of plus or minus moves one percent, then tap Go to this position to jump. After jumping, Return to before jump takes you back to where you were. Jumping stops any active read aloud session.

Settings

Open Settings from the bottom of the watch library. Appearance has separate controls for text size, font, page colour (dark or sepia), top and bottom margin, and side margin, each with a live preview. Changing the text size or margins re-flows the pages against the actual screen. Settings also lets you keep the screen awake while reading, set the pace for auto page turn, and adjust read aloud speed.

Adding books

The watch cannot download books by itself. In the phone library, tap the plus button to open Add book. You will find three cards: From a file (with a Choose file button), From a link, and Free books. Free books lets you search Project Gutenberg classics by title or author with a language filter; these books are public domain in the US, and readers elsewhere should check local copyright laws. While you have no books of your own, a Start with a free classic card offers three classics that download and send to your watch with a single tap.

WatchReader supports EPUB, FB2, .fb2.zip, and plain text files, and you can also share a book to WatchReader from other apps. On the phone, plain text encodings are detected automatically, including UTF-8, UTF-16, GB18030, Big5, Shift_JIS, and European and Cyrillic code pages. To send a book to your watch, open it and tap Send in the title bar, or use the three dots menu on the book cover and choose Send to watch.

Below is a passage in the public domain, so you have real typesetting to look at and a few paragraphs to hear read aloud.

Pride and Prejudice, the first chapter

It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife.

However little known the feelings or views of such a man may be on his first entering a neighbourhood, this truth is so well fixed in the minds of the surrounding families, that he is considered as the rightful property of some one or other of their daughters.

"My dear Mr. Bennet," said his lady to him one day, "have you heard that Netherfield Park is let at last?"

Mr. Bennet replied that he had not.

"But it is," returned she; "for Mrs. Long has just been here, and she told me all about it."

Mr. Bennet made no answer.

"Do you not want to know who has taken it?" cried his wife impatiently.

"You want to tell me, and I have no objection to hearing it."

This was invitation enough.

"Why, my dear, you must know, Mrs. Long says that Netherfield is taken by a young man of large fortune from the north of England; that he came down on Monday in a chaise and four to see the place, and was so much delighted with it that he agreed with Mr. Morris immediately; that he is to take possession before Michaelmas, and some of his servants are to be in the house by the end of next week."

"What is his name?"

"Bingley."

"Is he married or single?"

"Oh! single, my dear, to be sure! A single man of large fortune; four or five thousand a year. What a fine thing for our girls!"

That is the end of the sample. To delete it on the watch, long press the book in the watch library and choose Delete, which removes only the watch copy. To delete it on the phone, long press its cover or tap the three dots and choose Delete, which removes it from both the phone and the watch.
