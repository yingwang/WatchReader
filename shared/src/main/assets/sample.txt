Start here

This short guide comes with the app, on the phone and on the watch. It explains both, and it doubles as something to try every control on. When you are done with it, delete it and nothing else is affected.

Reading on the phone

Tap a book in the library and it opens here, laid out a page at a time. Tap the right half of the page to go forward and the left half to go back, the same way round as on the watch. Your place is kept, so the book opens where you left it.

Two buttons sit in the top right. The list opens the book's contents, when the book has chapters to jump between; this guide's own sections are listed there. The other sends the book to your watch. A book only needs sending once; after that the watch has its own copy and reads it without the phone nearby.

Reading on the watch

Tap the right half of the screen to go forward and the left half to go back. The crown does the same thing: turn it away from you to go forward, towards you to go back, one page for each notch.

Press and hold anywhere and a toolbar appears. It folds away on its own after a few seconds, or when you press and hold again.

Your place travels with you. Read a few pages on the watch and the phone opens at the same spot, and the other way round, so you can put the phone down and carry on from your wrist.

Reading aloud

Press the triangle in the toolbar and the watch reads the book to you with the system speech engine. The sentence being spoken is highlighted as it goes. Reading carries on when the screen turns off and when you leave the app, and the notification carries a pause and a stop. To stop, press the square in the toolbar or stop it from the notification.

You never have to choose a voice. Each sentence is spoken in the language it is written in, so a book in English and a book in Chinese both read correctly, and a book that mixes the two switches as it goes. The bottom of the settings screen tells you which languages this particular watch can speak. If one is missing, install its voice in the watch's own system settings.

Under the triangle is a jump bar: each press of plus or minus moves five percent, which is the quickest way back to the beginning.

Settings

Open Settings from the watch's library to change the text size, the typeface, the page colour and the reading speed. Changing the text size re-flows the pages against the real screen, so nothing is ever cut off at the round edge.

Adding books

The watch does not download books itself. Open WatchReader on the phone, press the plus button, and pick a .txt or .epub file or paste a link. You can also share a file to WatchReader from any other app.

Below is a passage in the public domain, so you have real typesetting to look at and a few paragraphs to hear read aloud.

Pride and Prejudice, the first chapter

It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife.

However little known the feelings or views of such a man may be on his first entering a neighbourhood, this truth is so well fixed in the minds of the surrounding families, that he is considered as the rightful property of some one or other of their daughters.

"My dear Mr. Bennet," said his lady to him one day, "have you heard that Netherfield Park is let at last?"

Mr. Bennet replied that he had not.

"But it is," returned she; "for Mrs. Long has just been here, and she told me all about it."

Mr. Bennet made no answer.

"Do you not want to know who has taken it?" cried his wife impatiently.

"You want to tell me, and I have no objection to hearing it."

This was invitation enough.

"Why, my dear, you must know, Mrs. Long says that Netherfield is taken by a young man of large fortune from the north of England; that he came down on Monday in a chaise and four to see the place, and was so much delighted with it that he agreed with Mr. Morris immediately; that he is to take possession before Michaelmas, and some of his servants are to be in the house by the end of next week."

"What is his name?"

"Bingley."

"Is he married or single?"

"Oh! single, my dear, to be sure! A single man of large fortune; four or five thousand a year. What a fine thing for our girls!"

That is the end of the sample. Swipe left to delete it, then add a book you actually want to read.
